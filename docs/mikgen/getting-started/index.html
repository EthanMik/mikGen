Getting Started

Build your first autonomous path in mikGen.

Selecting a Format​

To get started, first select the format you will be using. The format determines which segments are available, what they are named, and what constants they use; since mikGen matches your chosen library's internals. It is recommended to read your library's documentation:

  • mikLib
  • LemLib
  • JAR-Template
  • EZ-Template

If your robot is holonomic you can select that here:

important

Swapping formats will reset all your segment constants. See Supported Segments by Format for how the formats compare.

Building Your First Path​

To add the start of the path, left click on the field

You can then left click again to add a Drive to Point segment

The most common segments are added with these clicks:

ButtonSegmentDescription
Left ClickpointDriveDrives the robot to a desired (x, y)
Ctrl + Left ClickposeDriveDrives the robot to a desired (x, y, theta)
Right ClickpointTurnPoints the robot at the drive segment in front of it, or the field centre if there is none

Every segment and keybind is listed in Segment Interfacing.

A few tips while building:

  • Drag any waypoint on the field to move it. Hold Ctrl while dragging to snap to the grid.
  • New segments are inserted after the currently selected segment.

Editing a Segment​

Click a segment on the field or in the path list to select it.

Press Backspace to delete a segment and Ctrl+Z to undo.

Playing the Path​

To play your path, press spacebar or click the play button at the bottom of the screen

Copying the Code​

Once your path is ready, press Ctrl+Shift+C to copy the generated code for the whole path, or Ctrl+C to copy only the selected segments. The output uses your chosen library's API, so it can be pasted directly into your autonomous routine:

chassis.set_coordinates(0, 0, 0);
chassis.drive_to_point(24, 24);

mikGen can also write code directly into your project files. See Exporting & Saving.

Troubleshooting​

The most common issue in mikGen is your robot not matching up with the simulation in real life. Here are some reasons why:

IssueSolution
Odometry wheels have bad build quality; not banded down, not perpendicular, have high frictionMake sure odom point is mounted on a crossbrace, the wheel has two attachment points, and if a two inch odom pod is used, it is doubled and offseted from the other to cover the gaps. If you robot is very consistent with how much it differs from the website then this is likely not the issue.
Units are offMove the robot with a tape measure with odometry values printing, and make sure lateral and horizontal distance match the tape measures reading, just 0.1" difference over 1 foot adds up.
Offsets are offIf you turn the robot 180 degrees, your robot's position should not change, if it does then your offsets are wrong, and you need to remeasure
Not using odom wheelsMake sure you limit acceleration driving as it is very easy for the wheels to slip
Robot not started at right positionThis is the most common mistake. For optimal alignment, use the field tiles or walls as a reference point to where to place drivetrain. Then at the start of the program, set the odometry where the robot is in mikGen
important

Your robot will not match the website perfectly, there is usually a 1-2 tolerance in a full auton run. Correcting odom values with distance sensors helps a lot
